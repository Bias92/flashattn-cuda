# Holdout shapes, RTX 4060 Ti, 2026-09-20

> Superseded by `kernel_v7_2026-09-20_rtx4060ti.md`. The numbers below were measured on an
> intermediate kernel (v3) that was 3.7-7.9% slower than the committed one on causal inputs
> whose length is not a multiple of 64. Kept as a record of what was measured, not as a result.

Tile sizes and buffering were tuned only on B=1, H=8 or 32/8, N in {1024, 2048, 4096}.
The eight shapes below were fixed on 2026-09-19 before any of them was timed, and no tile or
buffering parameter was changed after that. One decision did see some of them: the strided
kernel had three candidate ways of computing K/V source addresses, and the A/B that picked one
ran on ten tuned cases and six of the sixteen below. The candidates differed by about 2% or
less on those six. They vary batch, head count, GQA ratio and length;
1000, 2500 and 3000 are not multiples of the 64-row tile, so those rows run the guarded path.

    python3 bench/compare_pytorch.py --set holdout

fp16, forward with L, the kernel that reads its inputs through strides (tests 66/66).
Three runs of 10 paired reps, implementations rotating within a rep; the table is the median
of the three runs, the last column the three per-run gaps against flash. Positive gap = custom
slower. torch 2.10.0+cu128. No other process used more than 15% of the GPU before or after a run.

## What changed since the first attempt at this table

A first pass on 2026-09-19 timed commit 32056b7 with a script that entered `sdpa_kernel` inside
the timed calls. That costs CPU time on the PyTorch side only, which can matter at about 0.1 ms
per call: B=8 N=512 causal read -19% then and swung between -8% and -26% across runs. The
backend is now selected once around each timing sample, and the same row is -8.1% with runs of
-8.1 / -7.7 / -9.0. That pass is not used anywhere.

`bench/compare_pytorch.py` takes its shapes from one table (`--set readme` or `--set holdout`)
and sizes each timing sample to about 60 ms of GPU time instead of a per-N iteration count.
The GQA and D=128 README shapes, which used to be measured with throwaway scripts, are part of
the `readme` set.
